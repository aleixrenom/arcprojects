# Rules

## Core concepts
- Epic fantasy ttrpg
- High player expression on builds
- Simple and agile
- RP-centric
- Damage matters, aggression is dangerous

## Stats
- There are three: Body (for physical stuff), Mind (for things that require learning and logical thinking), and Soul (for things that require less tangible understanding and intuition).
- These define who you are, your characteristics as a human.
- Each level in a stat gives you 1 more d6 to roll when you make a check with it.
- Each level on a stat gives you an additional d6 to use for checks using that stat.

### Body
[Preamble]
Here's some recommendations for how much weight a character can handle depending on its Body stat. You should be able to momentarily lift and move the weight corresponding to your Body level, reliably carry the amount one level below by using both hands and full attention, and effortlessly carry around the amount two levels below.
If an effect refers to "effective Body", use that number instead of the Body stat with these guidelines.

- **Body 1:** 30kg (check-in suitcase, two water buckets, medium dog)
- **Body 2:** 60kg (washing machine, two sacks of grain, adult wolf)
- **Body 3:** 120kg (loaded fridge, knight in full plate, adult deer stag)
- **Body 4:** 250kg (motorcycle, oak banquet table, lion)
- **Body 5:** 500kg (piano, armoured warhorse, grizzly bear)
- **Body 6:** 1000kg (small car, loaded cart, adult bull)
- **Body 7:** 2000kg (delivery van, castle portcullis, rhinoceros)

## Abilities
- These define what makes you special, what you are much better at than all the rest.
- Stats are generic, while abilities are specialised and apply to less situations.
- Each ability is linked to a stat.
- When you roll a check with an ability, you use that ability's dice pool instead of a stat for the roll, unless the ability states otherwise.
- Ability descriptions may have named and unnamed effects. If an ability has named effects, each one is a different way to use it: when you take an action with the ability, choose only one named effect unless stated otherwise. Unnamed effects are always active. Abilities with no named effects simply do what they say.
- Each level on an ability gives you an additional d6 to use for checks using that ability.
- There is Action abilities and Reaction abilities
	- Action abilities push the action forward and solve specialised and niche problems. In conflicts, they are the attacks and active actions you take against an opponent.
	- Reaction abilities help you resist against a move being made towards you, stand your ground, be less affected by change. In conflicts, they are the defences and the things you do to avoid a bad outcome that the opponent's action is trying to bring upon you.
- While stats are set in stone, abilities are infinitely varied and can allow you to do anything you can imagine. For that reason there should be default ability lists and ability lists that the GM comes up with, though players working with GMs to create an ability they want to have should always be encouraged.

### Topics
Some abilities grant Expertise in a **topic**. A topic is a field of practice or knowledge. something a person could plausibly be known for. "The athletic one", "the herbalist", "the ex-soldier" are all topics. "The one who deals with people" is not: that's not a field, it's a whole way of interacting with the world, and your stats already cover those.

#### Choosing a topic
- **Topics are nouns, not adjectives.** Fields, crafts, and histories make good topics (falconry, siegecraft, court etiquette, life as a smuggler). Personality traits do not (sneaky, charming, intimidating).
- **A topic sits inside a stat's domain, it doesn't mirror it.** Athletics is a slice of what Body does; "physical stuff" is just Body with extra steps. If a topic could replace the stat it's linked to, it's too broad.
- **If in doubt, be specific.** A narrow, evocative topic (haggling, reading a crowd, funerary rites) will come up more often than you fear and says more about your character than a broad one ever could.

#### Advice for the GM
- **The frequency test:** if a topic would plausibly apply to most checks in a typical session, it's too broad. Ask the player to narrow it down.
- **Narrow, don't refuse.** The answer to a too-broad topic is not "no", it's "which part?". "Dealing with people" can become interrogation, courtly etiquette, or reading a crowd, all legitimate and more flavourful than the broad version.
- **Say yes generously to narrow topics.** You are the final arbiter of whether a topic applies to a check. Players who pick specific or theoretical topics are telling you what kind of scenes they want, feed them those scenes.
- **Watch the collection, not just the topic.** A character accumulating many topics under one stat may each pass the tests individually while the collection starts mirroring the whole stat. You may veto a new topic if the pile is becoming a stat in disguise.
- **New topics crystallise, they don't appear.** When a character gains a topic at level-up, treat it as knowledge or practice that has been building in the background and has now clicked into place. If that strains belief, you may ask to wait for the next Downtime before the new topic becomes usable.

#### Sidebar version
**What's a topic?** A topic is a field of practice or knowledge, something a person could plausibly be known for (falconry, siegecraft, court etiquette). Topics are nouns, not adjectives, and a topic covers a slice of a stat's domain, never the whole thing. GM: if a topic would apply to most checks in a session, ask the player to narrow it: the fix is "which part?", not "no".

## Health and Resolve
- There is two pools that, if depleted, take you out of the action: health and resolve. 
- Health represents your physical state, and will lower as you take hits.
	- It is calculated as 3 plus your Body level.
- Resolve represents your mental and inner fortitude, the ability to whitstand magical attacks that affect your mind, curses, and even tense negotiations and social interactions.
	- It is calculated as 3 plus the highest one between your Mind or Soul levels.
- When any of these two pools reaches 0, you are out of the scene and your character can't act for themselves. You will have to be rescued by someone or fall to the grasp of your enemies.
- Both pools *can* go into the negative, requiring more healing and/or Rests for you to recover. You will remain unable to play as your character normally as long as any pool is at 0 or below. This can be represented with your character being in a coma, deeply shaken, or otherwise recovering from grave punishment. 

### Recovery
Keep noted next to the health and resolve pools the "recovery" number, which is always a third of the pool rounded up. That is the amount of health/resolve you recover on a Rest (see the "rhythm of play" section for the definition of Rest).

### About death
Death is not a consequence of the mechanics, it's a consequence of the narrative. 
By default reaching 0 on health or resolve doesn't kill you, just makes you lose control of your character until they can recover. The only way your character can die if you and the GM have agreed to it and it makes sense in the narrative. 
So be sure to talk to your GM about it! 
## Checks
- A check is the act of rolling a number of dice, with the objective to get a number of successes that equal or surpass a difficulty number.
- A 4, 5 or 6 on a die gives you +1 success
- You have a number of dice pools, that are the dice that will be rolled when rolling checks. For example if you are rolling a Body check, you roll the amount of dice that you have in your Body stat dice pool, which is equal to its level.
- As described in the Abilities section, if you roll an ability check, you use the dice that are in the ability dice pool plus the ones on the dice pool of the stat linked to that ability.

### Check difficulties
Every check needs to have a difficulty, which is the amount of successes on the roll that will be needed to pass it, and what will indicate if it is a close call or not.
(ToDo: playtest difficulties for guidance after defining character creation)
### Close calls
- A close call happens when you roll a number of successes exactly equal to the difficulty level, one above, or one below.
- They represent the tension of nearly succeeding or nearly failing on an action, and the uncertainty that comes with that.
- When this happens, count the number of 1s and 6s you rolled.
	- If you rolled more 6s than 1s, you get the difference in boons.
	- If you rolled more 1s than 6s, you get the difference in complications.
	- If you rolled the same amount or none, you get an insight token.
- Boons, complications and insight tokens are described below. Notice that they can happen regardless of if you failed or succeeded on the check. 

**Boon:** a good thing that comes out of the check (partial success on a fail, someone is impressed on a success...)
**Complication:** a bad thing that comes out of the check (partial failure on a success, your tool breaks on a failure, your rival angers on a success...)
**Insight token:** until the end of the session, you can spend an insight token to change any die rolled by anyone into your choice of a 1 or a 6.

### Contested checks
Two characters can have contested checks simply by rolling the appropriate dice pools at the same time, each character's amount of successes serving as the other character's check difficulty number.
This may trigger close calls. If both characters get boons and complications, the GM may decide that one's boons cancel the other's, same for complications.

## Conflicts
There is two sides on a conflict. Whoever starts the conflict will take the initiative, activate actions against the other side, then the other side will take actions in retaliation and take the initiative.

### Flow
1. Side A starts the conflict, taking the initiative. This switches the action from real-time to turn-based.
2. Everyone in side A may take 1 action.
3. Everyone in side B may take reactions.
4. Everyone in side B may take 1 action as retaliation.
5. Everyone in side A may take reactions.
6. Side B decides if they want to continue the conflict.

**If side B continues the conflict**
5. Everyone in side B may take 1 action.
6. Everyone in side A may take reactions.
7. Everyone in side A may take 1 action as retaliation.
8. Everyone in side B may take reactions.
9. Side A decides if they want to continue the conflict.

**If side B did not continue the conflict**
5. The action switches from turn-based to real-time. Note that side A still has the option to start the conflict anew if side B doesn't do anything to avoid that.

**Important to remember:** An action can always be answered by a reaction. If multiple actions are being directed at you, you can answer each of them with the same or different reactions.

### Damage
- During a conflict, an action may deal damage.
- The action will specify if it deals damage to health or to resolve.
- Usually, the action will involve a roll, and the amount of successes on that roll will determine the amount of damage that the aggressor would deal.
- The defender then may mitigate some or all the damage though one of their reactions.
- Any remaining damage connects, and reduces the specified pool (health or resolve) by the amount of damage dealt. Then if that pool reached 0, the defender is out of the scene.

### Conflicts as contests
Conflicts may also take the form of a series of contested checks, which may happen, for example, in tense negotiations, complicated social situations, or even contests of skill and showmanship.
During these, the GM may choose to make the winner of each contested check deal Resolve or Health (depending on the contest) damage to its loser equal to the difference of successes between them.

### Close calls during conflicts
Close calls activate during conflicts depending on the situation:
- They activate normally if the roll involves a check with a difficulty
- If you take 1 damage after a roll
- If you deal 1 damage after a roll

The complications that the opponent gets when they get a close call may benefit you.

### No matching reaction
If damage is coming towards you and you have no reaction ability to answer it, you will take the damage in full. So be sure to pick your battles wisely.

### Reasoning
Both the aggressor and the defender get a benefit: the aggressor gets to have the first strike, potentially reducing resources from the defender, while the defender potentially gets 2 actions before the aggressor can have their next action. 
A trade-off that will make people think if it's worth it or not to initiate a conflict.

## Rhythm of play
The majority of the action will happen in real time RP. During those times there may be checks with lesser or moderate tension. Those checks should generally be made with stats, with only very specific checks being made with abilities.

Then, when the tension truly rises, the stakes get higher, and adrenaline starts pumping, you should use conflicts. During conflicts you can only voluntarily activate Action abilities, which will make abilities the protagonist of conflicts. Remember that no-one is forced to act during conflicts, so if a player wants to do something that would require stats and not abilities to do, they can choose to not act during the conflict and act after it, all sides will always be able to choose to end a conflict early. 

### Scene, Rest, and Downtime
Mechanical effects and narrative may refer to these terms to mark recovery of resources and the passing of time.

#### Scene
A scene is a stretch of the story that happens in one place, in continuous time, around one situation. When the group moves elsewhere, time skips forward, or the situation holding the scene together resolves, the scene ends. A scene may contain a conflict; the conflict ending doesn't end the scene. The GM has the final call on when a scene ends, and per-scene effects can't be refreshed by briefly stepping away and coming back.

#### Rest
A Rest is a period of inactivity lasting a whole night or a whole day. Inactivity means no strenuous action and no lingering tension; the GM has the final call on whether a stretch of time counts. A normal day naturally offers up to two Rests: the night, and a day spent idle. Rests are consecutive if you use no abilities between them (you're resting, after all). Doing anything more than living quietly breaks the chain.

#### Downtime
Downtime is a stretch of the story, days or longer, where nothing presses on the characters and time passes in summary rather than in scenes. It's where the slow things happen: training, crafting, recovering what a Rest can't fix, and knowledge crystallising into new topics. The GM declares when downtime starts and ends. 

#### Session: the odd one out
Scenes, Rests, and Downtime are all measured inside the story. One thing is deliberately not: insight tokens expire at the end of the session, the real-world play meeting. This is on purpose. Insight tokens are tension at the table, not a resource in the world. Spend them while the night is hot, they don't keep. 

## Progression
- You get skill points regularly. The pace depends on the campaign, it could be one skill point every game session, one skill point every in-game day, multiple skill points gained during Downtime to represent training...
- You can use those skill points to improve stats and abilities
- The cost in points of a stat level or ability level is equal to its level, so unlocking an ability at lvl1 costs 1 skill point, upgrading a stat to lvl3 costs 3 skill points, etc.
- You can't buy the same ability more than once unless stated otherwise.

# Character creation
You get 18 skill points to obtain and level up stats and abilities. You don't have to spend them all, you can save unused ones to level up more expensive stats or abilities in the future.

**Starting stats**
All three stats start at level 0, meaning that you don't have any dice in that stat's dice pool to use for checks. If that stat is still at 0 after character creation, it would represent your character's inability to interact with the world with that aspect of themselves (disabilities, accidents, etc.). If you have levels in an ability linked to a stat that you have at level 0, you can still use that ability, likely manifesting through magical means instead of physical ones.
